<h1>Chicken Noodle Soup</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">From the carcass, 3 hr mostly unattended, serves 4</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">The stock</span>
chicken carcass  1, stripped
onion            1, halved
carrot           1
celery           1 stick
bay leaves       2
peppercorns      1 tsp
water            to cover

<span style="color: #888;">The soup</span>
ginger           thumb, sliced
garlic           3 cloves, smashed
star anise       1
soy sauce        3 tbsp
egg noodles      200g
pak choi         2 heads, or any greens
spring onions    4, sliced
cooked chicken   150g, shredded
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Day three of the roast chicken. Makes 3 to 4 bowls, which is what 1.5 litres of stock gets you.<br>Throw a pack of raw wings in with the carcass if you have them. Stock from a roasted carcass is weaker than from raw bones, so the wings fix that and give you more meat.<br>One star anise, not two. More than that and it tastes medicinal.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Everything for the stock into the pot. Cover with cold water. No salt:

{{< ingr "chicken carcass=1 stripped, onion=1 halved, carrot=1, celery=1 stick, bay leaves=2, peppercorns=1 tsp, water=to cover" >}}

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Never salt stock. You season the soup at the end, and if the stock reduces there is no way back.</span>

**2.** Bring it up to barely a simmer, a few bubbles breaking the surface every couple of seconds. Three hours.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Do it in the oven, not on the hob, 110°C (90°C fan) with the lid on. The oven holds steady for hours so it can't drift into a boil, and the heat comes from all sides so nothing catches. Check after 30 minutes and drop the temperature if it is bubbling hard. A slow cooker does the same job.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Never let it boil, and don't stir it. A hard boil churns the fat back in and shears the scum into particles too fine to strain out. Left still, the scum stays on top as a raft and the sieve catches it, which is why there is no skimming in this recipe.</span>

**3.** Strain, and bin everything solid.

**4.** Stock back in the pot and simmer 10 minutes:

{{< ingr "ginger=thumb sliced, garlic=3 cloves smashed, star anise=1, soy sauce=3 tbsp" >}}

**5.** Noodles in their own pan.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Cook them separately. In the broth they drink it and go soggy, and any leftovers turn to porridge overnight.</span>

**6.** Greens into the broth for 2 minutes. Chicken in off the heat.

**7.** Noodles into the bowls, broth over the top, spring onions on.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">If you are not eating it the same day, chill the stock and lift the fat off as a solid disc. Easier and far more thorough than chasing it round a hot pot with a ladle.<br>If the stock comes out cloudy, ignore it. Clarity only matters for clear soups.<br>For the plain version, skip the ginger, star anise and soy. Sliced carrot, parsley and a big squeeze of lemon instead.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 3px solid #8fcfe8; padding-left: 0.6em;">❄ <strong>Freezer</strong><br>Freeze: the stock only, flat in bags, 6 months. Never with the noodles in.<br>Reheat: from frozen in a pan, then build the soup fresh from step 4.</span>
