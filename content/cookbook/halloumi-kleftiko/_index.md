<h1>Halloumi Kleftiko</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">Cypriot halloumi and potato bake, one pot, 1.5 hr, serves 4</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">In the pot</span>
potatoes         1.2kg, in chunks
chickpeas        1 x 400g tin, drained
red onions       2, in wedges
red peppers      2, in thick strips
garlic           1 bulb, cloves separated
cherry tomatoes  300g
halloumi         250g, thick slices
bay leaves       3
vegetable stock  150ml

<span style="color: #888;">The dressing</span>
olive oil        4 tbsp
lemon            1, juice and zest
dried oregano    1 tbsp
ground cinnamon  1/2 tsp
salt and pepper  to season

<span style="color: #888;">To finish</span>
lemon            1/2, to squeeze
dried mint       to scatter
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The veg version of <a href="/cookbook/kleftiko/">kleftiko</a>, halloumi in place of the lamb. Same Cypriot flavours, an hour and a half instead of the long braise.<br>Go light on the salt, halloumi brings plenty. Watch the stock too, use a low salt one or water.<br>A splash of white wine in place of some of the stock adds depth if you have a bottle open.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Heat the oven to 180°C. Mix the dressing:

{{< ingr "olive oil=4 tbsp, lemon=1 juice and zest, dried oregano=1 tbsp, ground cinnamon=1/2 tsp, salt and pepper=light" >}}

**2.** Tumble into the Dutch oven and toss in the dressing. Pour in the stock and tuck in the bay:

{{< ingr "potatoes=1.2kg in chunks, chickpeas=1 tin drained, red onions=2 in wedges, red peppers=2 in strips, garlic=1 bulb separated" >}}

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The chickpeas are what make it a full main rather than a side. Butter beans instead for creamier, or a tin of each for both textures. A splash more stock if the pot looks dry.</span>

**3.** Lid on, into the oven for 45 minutes, until the potatoes take a knife with a little resistance.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">If they are still hard, give them another 10 minutes with the lid on before the next step. The potatoes set the pace here.</span>

**4.** Scatter in the cherry tomatoes and lay the halloumi slices on top. Lid off, 25 to 30 minutes, until the halloumi is golden and the potatoes have crisp edges.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Halloumi on at the end, not the start. Baked the whole time it goes rubbery, tucked in late it turns golden and holds its bite.</span>

**5.** Rest 10 minutes. Squeeze over the lemon and scatter with dried mint. Serve from the pot with a Greek salad and bread.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Sources: <a href="https://www.thelastfoodblog.com/halloumi-tray-bake/">The Last Food Blog</a>, <a href="https://realgreekrecipes.com/kleftiko/">Real Greek Recipes</a>. Adapted from the lamb <a href="/cookbook/kleftiko/">kleftiko</a>.</span>
