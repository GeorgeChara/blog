<h1>Fermented Hot Sauce</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">2/4 weeks ferment, keeps a year in the fridge</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">Makes 1 bottle</span>

red chillies       300g
garlic             4 cloves
filtered water     500ml
sea salt           18g
white wine vinegar 2 tbsp
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Use filtered or bottled water. Chlorine in tap water stalls the ferment.<br>Mix of chillies works well. Deseed some for less heat, the ferment mellows it anyway.<br>The vinegar goes in at the end and helps it keep longer.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Wear gloves. Slice the chillies and peel the garlic. Deseed the chillies if you want it milder.

**2.** Pack the chillies and garlic into a clean jar.

**3.** Stir the salt into the water until dissolved. Pour over the chillies to cover them completely, leaving 2cm of space at the top.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:-0.5em; margin-bottom:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Keep everything submerged. Use a small weight or a folded cabbage leaf to hold the chillies under the brine. Anything above the surface can mould.</span>

**4.** Loosely cover and leave at room temperature, out of direct sun, for 2/4 weeks. Burp the jar daily by loosening the lid to release the gas. It is ready when it tastes pleasantly sour and the bubbling has slowed.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:-0.5em; margin-bottom:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">3/4 weeks gives the best flavour. A white film on top is harmless kahm yeast, just skim it. Pink, orange or fuzzy mould means start again.</span>

**5.** Blend the chillies and garlic with enough of the brine to reach the consistency you want. Stir in the vinegar.

**6.** Bottle and keep in the fridge. The flavour deepens over the first couple of months.
