<h1>Giouvetsi</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">Greek beef and orzo bake, one pot, 2 hr, serves 6</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">The stew</span>
diced beef       1kg, shoulder or shin
red onions       2, finely chopped
carrots          2, sliced
chopped tomatoes 1 x 400g tin
tomato puree     2 tbsp
red wine         150ml
cinnamon stick   1
caster sugar     1 tsp
olive oil        4 tbsp
salt and pepper  to season

<span style="color: #888;">The pasta</span>
orzo             500g
hot stock        about 1 litre
kefalotyri       100g, grated (or feta)
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Orzo (kritharaki) is a rice-shaped pasta. It cooks right in the sauce at the end, soaking up the flavour, so do not boil it separately.<br>Keep an eye on the liquid once the orzo goes in. It wants to be just covered and a little soupy, as it firms up as it sits.<br>Kefalotyri is the classic grating cheese. Feta crumbled over, or even halloumi, does the job.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Pat the beef dry. Warm half the oil in the Dutch oven over medium-low heat and soften the onions and carrots for 5 minutes.

**2.** Turn the heat up, push the veg aside, and brown the beef all over.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Dry meat and a hot pot. Crowd it and it stews instead of browning.</span>

**3.** Stir in the tomato puree for a minute, pour in the wine, and let it bubble away.

**4.** Add the tomatoes, cinnamon, sugar, and enough water to just cover the meat. Season. Lid on, and simmer gently for about 45 minutes, until the beef is nearly tender.

**5.** Heat the oven to 180°C. Toast the orzo in the rest of the oil in a frying pan until golden, then stir it into the pot. Pour in enough hot stock to cover the orzo by a finger, about a litre.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Toasting the orzo first keeps the grains separate and nutty rather than gluey.</span>

**6.** Lid on, into the oven for 30 minutes. Lid off, stir, add a splash more stock if it looks dry, and give it a final 15 minutes.

**7.** Rest a few minutes, pull out the cinnamon stick, and serve with the cheese grated over.
