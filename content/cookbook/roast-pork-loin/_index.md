<h1>Roast Pork Loin</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">with crackling, 240°C then 180°C, serves 4/6</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;">pork loin joint   1.5kg
oil               1 tbsp
flaky salt        2 tsp
pepper            to taste
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Loin is lean, the opposite of shoulder. Roast it to a temperature, never slow-cook it or it dries out.<br>The joint comes pre-tied and often pre-scored. Leave the string on to roast, snip it off before carving.<br>Serve with apple sauce, roast potatoes and greens.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Heat the oven to 240°C. Pat the rind really dry. If it is not already scored, score the fat in lines about 1cm apart, down to the meat but not into it. Rub all over with the oil, then the salt, working it into the scores.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:-0.5em; margin-bottom:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Optional, for better crackling: leave the joint uncovered in the fridge overnight first to dry the rind. It is the single biggest improvement. Pouring a kettle of boiling water over the rind before salting opens the scores too, but that is a marginal gain.<br>From frozen, the easy plan: night before, move it from the freezer to the fridge. Next day, once the fat has thawed enough to cut, score it, pat the rind dry and leave it uncovered in the fridge overnight, that finishes the thaw and dries the rind in one go. On the cooking day, oil and salt then roast. Leave the oil and salt for the day, you want the rind drying out bare overnight. Just check it is thawed right through before it goes in.</span>

**2.** Sit the joint rind up in a roasting tin. Blast at 240°C for 25 minutes, until the crackling starts to bubble and crisp.

**3.** Turn the oven down to 180°C and roast for roughly 30 minutes per 500g, until a thermometer reads 65°C in the centre. Go by the thermometer, not the clock.

**4.** Rest under loose foil for 15 minutes, it climbs a few more degrees as it sits. Snip off the string and carve.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Gravy: roast it on a bed of halved onions and garlic, then make gravy from the pan juices.<br>Porchetta style: untie, rub the inside with garlic, fennel seeds, rosemary and lemon zest, re-roll and tie. Same method.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 3px solid #8fcfe8; padding-left: 0.6em;"><span style="color:#3f9fd4; font-size:1.1em;">❄ <strong>Freezer</strong></span><br>Freeze: leftover slices in a bag with a little of the juices.<br>Reheat: gently in gravy or stock so the lean meat does not dry out.<br>Keeps: 2/3 months frozen, 3 days in the fridge.</span>
