<h1>Mushroom Bourguignon</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">Vegan, one pot, 1 hr, serves 4</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">The stew</span>
chestnut mushrooms  600g, halved
shallots            250g, peeled and whole
carrots             3, in thick chunks
garlic              3 cloves, sliced
red wine            250ml
vegetable stock     400ml
tomato puree        2 tbsp
plain flour         2 tbsp
olive oil           2 tbsp

<span style="color: #888;">The aromatics</span>
thyme               few sprigs
bay leaves          2
salt and pepper     to season
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Chestnut mushrooms have the most flavour. A handful of dried porcini soaked in the stock pushes it deeper.<br>Small shallots or frozen pearl onions save the peeling. Whole, so they stay intact in the braise.<br>A wine good enough to drink. Cheap and harsh shows in the sauce.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Heat half the oil in the Dutch oven over medium high heat. Brown the mushrooms hard, in batches, until deeply coloured. Lift them out.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Do not crowd the pot or they steam and go grey. The dark sear is where the meaty flavour comes from.</span>

**2.** Rest of the oil in, then soften for 8 minutes:

{{< ingr "shallots=250g whole, carrots=3, garlic=3 cloves sliced" >}}

**3.** Stir in the tomato puree for a minute, then the flour for another. Pour in the wine and let it bubble down by half, scraping the bottom of the pot.

**4.** Mushrooms back in, then lid on and simmer 30 minutes until the carrots are tender and the sauce is glossy:

{{< ingr "vegetable stock=400ml, thyme=few sprigs, bay leaves=2" >}}

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">It should end as a thick gravy that coats the mushrooms. If it is still thin, lid off for the last 10 minutes.</span>

**5.** Season and pull out the bay. Serve over <a href="/cookbook/mashed-potato/">mashed potato</a> to catch the sauce.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Better the next day, like any braise.<br>For hands off, do the braise at step 4 with the lid on in the oven at 160°C (140°C fan) for 40 minutes.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 3px solid #8fcfe8; padding-left: 0.6em;">❄ <strong>Freezer</strong><br>Freeze: cool fully, freeze in portions for up to 3 months.<br>Reheat: from frozen in a pan on low, a splash of water if it tightens.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Sources: <a href="https://www.noracooks.com/mushroom-bourguignon/">Nora Cooks</a>, <a href="https://simple-veganista.com/mushroom-bourguignon_10/">The Simple Veganista</a>.</span>

Sources compared:
  1. Nora Cooks         16oz mushrooms, 1.5 cups wine, flour+water slurry, hob
  2. Simple Veganista   2lb mushrooms, pearl onions, wine reduced by half,
                        Dutch oven, flour+oil paste

Where they disagree:
  - Wine: Nora 1.5 cups (~350ml), Veganista 1 cup reduced. Went 250ml reduced,
    reducing it concentrates and loses the raw edge, better than more unreduced.
  - Onions: Veganista uses pearl onions, Nora just diced. Used whole small
    shallots, they hold shape in the braise like the pearl onions but are easier
    to buy. Flagged frozen pearl onions in the note.
  - Thickening: both thicken with flour. Toasted the flour into the sofrito
    (step 3) rather than a slurry at the end, cleaner and no raw-flour taste.
  - Mushroom amount: 600g for 4 sits between the two.

Both are hob. Added the oven option since that is your usual braise method.
Vegan as written. Serve with mash, or their mashed-potato recipe (cross-linked).
