<h1>Farata</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">60% hydration, 1 hr, Mauritian flaky flatbread, makes 6</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">Makes 6</span>

plain flour   400g
ghee          for layering
warm water    240g
oil           2 tbsp
salt          6g
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Almost the same dough as <a href="/cookbook/flour-tortillas/">flour tortillas</a>. The difference is the ghee folded through, which makes it flaky.<br>Ghee is traditional. Melted butter works.<br>Serve with <a href="/cookbook/cari-poulet/">cari poulet</a>.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Mix:

{{< ingr "flour=400g, salt=6g, oil=2 tbsp" >}}

Add the warm water gradually to a soft, smooth dough. Knead 5 minutes. Cover and rest 30 minutes.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:-0.5em; margin-bottom:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Stand mixer: dough hook on speed 2 for 3/4 minutes until smooth.</span>

**2.** Divide into 6 balls. Roll each into a thin circle on a floured surface.

**3.** Brush the circle all over with melted ghee. Make a cut from the centre to the edge, then roll the dough up tightly into a cone from one side of the cut to the other. Coil the cone into a tight spiral and tuck the end under. Repeat for all 6.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:-0.5em; margin-bottom:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The coiling is what builds the flaky layers. More ghee and tighter coiling means flakier farata.</span>

**4.** Cover the spirals and rest 15 minutes. Then roll each one out thin again into a circle.

**5.** Heat a pan over medium. Cook each farata 2 minutes a side, brushing with ghee, until golden with brown spots and slightly puffed.

**6.** As each comes off the pan, scrunch it gently between your hands to separate the layers. Keep warm wrapped in a tea towel.
