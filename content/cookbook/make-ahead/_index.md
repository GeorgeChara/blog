<h1>Make-Ahead Prep</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">A component-based batch-prep and storage guide</p>

The core principle: **freeze bases and components, not finished meals.** A tub of <a href="/cookbook/ragu/">ragù</a> is far more useful than a frozen lasagne, it can become pasta, lasagne, a bake, or a jacket topping depending on the night. You assemble fresh; the freezer just removes the slow steps. The only true grab-and-go items are the burritos.

---

## Core Principles

**Components, not ready-meals**
Freeze the base (ragù, braise, sauce, marinated protein), cook the quick fresh part (pasta, rice, a seared protein, a tortilla) on the night. This saves freezer space, frees your oven dishes, and keeps every dinner an open decision rather than a fixed meal.

**The freezer-safe test**
- *Cooked, reduced, low-moisture* freezes well (braises, sauces, fillings, blanched components).
- *Fresh, dairy, or emulsified* (sour cream, fresh salsa, guac, cheese sauce, ranch) never freeze; add at the table or assemble fresh.
- *Vinegar/sugar sauces* (brown sauce, ketchup, reduced BBQ) freeze fine, used sparingly.

**Quality window**
Frozen food stays *safe* almost indefinitely at -18°C, but *quality* peaks at 2/3 months (sauces and braises stretch to 6). Cook on a 2/4 week horizon so nothing tastes freezer-tired. Label everything with date and contents.

**Kill the air**
Freezer burn comes from trapped air. Press flat, squeeze out air, freeze flat, then stack like files.

**~15 portions a session, max**
Past that, prep stops being fun. Better to double a base whenever you're already cooking than run one giant marathon.

**Cook once, use many ways**
One braise becomes a main, then a wrap, then a frittata or topping. Variety without extra cooking.

## Burritos, Your Grab-and-Go

The one place finished items earn their freezer space, because they reheat perfectly and skip all weekday assembly. Roll tight, wrap individually in baking paper or foil, freeze. **Reheat: 90s microwave, or 60s microwave then a minute in a dry pan to crisp.**

**Rules:** dry fillings only. Cook mushrooms hard, squeeze spinach, cool everything before rolling (steam means sogginess). Beans and refried beans act as a moisture buffer. Sauce: cooked/reduced or vinegar-based goes *in* (thin smear); fresh and dairy goes on at the table.

### Breakfast burritos

**Classic:**
1. Egg, cheese, roast veg, <a href="/cookbook/jalapeno-relish/">jalapeño relish</a>
2. Bean, potato, egg *(beans = moisture buffer)*
3. Bacon, egg, potato
4. Mushroom, spinach, feta

**Bolder:**
5. Chorizo, egg, potato, chipotle-tomato *(the standout)*
6. Halloumi, egg, roast pepper, harissa-tomato
7. Sausage, egg, caramelised onion, mustard-cheese
8. Full English: bacon, sausage, egg, beans, black pudding *(plus a thin smear of brown sauce, vinegar-based, freezes fine)*
9. Black bean, sweet potato, egg, cooked salsa verde *(healthiest)*

### Lunch burritos and wraps

Sturdier, savoury, built off your frozen bases:
1. <a href="/cookbook/carnitas/">Carnitas</a>, rice, slaw, chipotle-tomato
2. Chilli con carne, rice, cheese
3. <a href="/cookbook/ragu/">Ragù</a>, grilled veg *(uses the base)*
4. Chicken fajita, peppers, cheese *(assemble from the fajita kit)*
5. Halloumi, roast veg, harissa-tomato *(vegetarian)*

### Lunch and snack pre-mades (non-burrito)

A couple of grab staples beyond wraps:
- **<a href="/cookbook/sausage-rolls/">Sausage rolls</a> / cheese-and-onion rolls**, freeze unbaked, bake from frozen 25/30 min. Best fresh-baked snack there is.
- **Empanadas / pasties**, any leftover braise (ragù, chilli, carnitas) sealed in pastry, frozen raw, baked from frozen.
- **Frittata muffins**, eggs, roast veg, cheese in a muffin tin, baked, frozen. Reheat 60s. Protein-heavy, no bread.
- **Savoury muffins / cheese scones**, freeze baked, thaw at room temp or warm briefly.
- **Soup portions**, the easiest desk lunch; reheat from frozen.

## Baby Weaning, Freezer Basics (from ~6 months)

Classic first-stage purées and soft foods, all of which freeze brilliantly in ice-cube trays (about 1 cube = 1 portion for a new eater). Cook, blend smooth, cool fast, freeze in trays, then pop cubes into labelled bags. Thaw a cube or two in the fridge overnight or gently in a pan; reheat until piping hot, then **cool to lukewarm before serving** and never refreeze a thawed portion.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Item | Freezer | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sweet potato purée | 3 months | The classic first food, naturally sweet, smooth, well tolerated. Steam or roast, blend. |
| Butternut squash / carrot purée | 3 months | As above. Loosen with a little water, milk, or their usual milk. |
| Pea / broccoli / spinach purée | 3 months | Green veg early helps acceptance of bitter flavours. Blend smooth, sieve if stringy. |
| Apple / pear purée | 3 months | Stew soft, blend. Mix into veg or porridge later. |
| Baby porridge cubes | 1 month | Oats cooked with milk, blended; freeze in cubes, loosen on reheat. |
| Blended family base (no salt) | 3 months | Reserve a portion of your braise/stew/ragù **before salting**, blend smooth. Lets baby eat a version of the family meal. |
| Lentil / dahl purée (mild) | 3 months | Iron and protein. No salt, mild or no spice at first; build up gradually. |

</div>

**Key rules for baby prep:**
- **No added salt** (their kidneys can't handle it) and **no honey** under 12 months (botulism risk). Reserve unsalted portions of family food *before* seasoning.
- **Cool fast, freeze fresh, reheat once, piping hot, then cool to serve.** Never refreeze thawed baby food.
- Ice-cube trays are the perfect portion size early on; move to small tubs as appetite grows.
- The unsalted "family base" trick is the long-game win, one pot feeds everyone, baby just gets theirs blended and unseasoned.

*General guidance only, follow your health visitor and NHS Start4Life for timing, textures, and allergen introduction.*
