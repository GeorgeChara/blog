<h1>Tahdig</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">Persian rice with a crisp crust, 1 hr, serves 4</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;">basmati rice     400g
butter           75g
oil              3 tbsp
yoghurt          3 tbsp
saffron          pinch, in 3 tbsp hot water
salt             to season
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The Le Creuset does this better than anything else. Heavy even base, no hot spots.<br>Tahdig means bottom of the pot. The crust is the dish, the fluffy rice above it is the side.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then soak 30 minutes in salted water.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Rinsing washes off loose starch. Skip it and the grains clag together.</span>

**2.** Boil in plenty of salted water for 5 minutes, until a grain bends but still has a hard bite in the middle. Drain.

**3.** Mix two ladles of the rice with the yoghurt and half the saffron water:

{{< ingr "parboiled rice=2 ladles, yoghurt=3 tbsp, saffron water=half" >}}

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The yoghurt is what makes the crust golden and crisp rather than just brown.</span>

**4.** Melt the butter and oil in the pot, then spread the yoghurt rice flat across the bottom.

**5.** Pile the rest of the rice on top in a mound. Poke a few holes down through it with the handle of a spoon.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The holes let steam escape so the top cooks evenly.</span>

**6.** Wrap the lid in a tea towel, put it on, and cook on the lowest heat for 30 to 45 minutes.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The tea towel catches the condensation. Without it, water drips back down and the crust goes soggy.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Start checking at 30. You should hear it crackling and smell toasted rice, not burnt. Cast iron holds more heat than the pans most recipes assume, so it can be ready sooner.</span>

**7.** Run a knife round the edge and turn the whole thing out onto a plate, crust up. Pour the rest of the saffron water over.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Eat the day you make it. The crust doesn't survive the fridge.</span>
