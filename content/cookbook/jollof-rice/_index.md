<h1>Jollof Rice</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">Nigerian party jollof, 1 hr, Dutch oven, serves 6</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">Pepper base</span>
plum tomatoes      400g tin
red peppers        2
onion              1
garlic             3 cloves, optional
ginger             1 inch, optional
scotch bonnet      1

<span style="color: #888;">Rice</span>
parboiled rice     500g
onion              1
tomato puree       3 tbsp
vegetable oil      100ml
thyme              1 tsp
curry powder       1 tsp
bay leaves         2
stock cubes        2
water              400ml
salt               to taste
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Use long grain parboiled rice. It holds its shape, normal rice goes mushy.<br>The scotch bonnet brings the heat. Deseed it or use half for a milder result.<br>Maggi or Knorr cubes are the authentic seasoning.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Blend the tinned tomatoes, red peppers, 1 onion and the scotch bonnet to a smooth puree. Add the garlic and ginger too if using.

**2.** Heat the oil in the Dutch oven. Dice the other onion and fry until soft. Add the tomato puree and cook 2/3 minutes.

**3.** Pour in the blended pepper base. Cook it down for 15/20 minutes, stirring, until thick, deep red and the raw smell is gone. This is the most important step.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:-0.5em; margin-bottom:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Cooking the base down properly is what makes jollof taste like jollof. Rush it and it stays sour and thin.</span>

**4.** Stir in the thyme, curry powder, bay leaves, crumbled stock cubes and salt.

**5.** Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then add to the pot with the water. Stir to coat. The liquid should just cover the rice.

**6.** Lid on tight, lowest heat, and cook 25/30 minutes until the rice is tender and the liquid absorbed. Don't stir, let the bottom catch slightly for the smoky flavour.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:-0.5em; margin-bottom:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">A sheet of foil under the lid seals the steam in for evenly cooked rice. The slightly crispy bottom layer is the prize, not a mistake.</span>

**7.** Fluff with a fork and serve.
