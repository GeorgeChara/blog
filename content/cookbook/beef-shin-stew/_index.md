<h1>Beef Shin Stew</h1>
<p style="color: #888; margin-top: -0.5em;">Beef shin braised in red wine, one pot, 3.5 hr, serves 6</p>

## Ingredients

<pre style="padding: 1em; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block; margin: 0; color: #000;"><span style="color: #888;">The stew</span>
beef shin        1kg, in big chunks
onions           2, chopped
carrots          3, in thick chunks
celery           2 sticks, chopped
garlic           4 cloves, sliced
red wine         300ml
beef stock       500ml
tomato puree     2 tbsp
olive oil        2 tbsp

<span style="color: #888;">The aromatics</span>
bay leaves       2
thyme            few sprigs
salt and pepper  to season

<span style="color: #888;">To finish</span>
potatoes         800g, halved, optional
</pre>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Ask for shin by name. Supermarkets often label it braising steak or stewing beef, and the packaged stewing beef is lean and cut unevenly.<br>Chuck is the easiest swap. Around £9 to £11 a kilo at a butcher.<br>Keep the bones if you get it bone in. The marrow goes into the sauce.</span>

## Recipe

**1.** Heat the oven to 130°C. Pat the beef dry and salt it.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Dry meat browns, wet meat steams.</span>

**2.** Brown the beef hard in the oil in the Dutch oven, in batches, until properly dark. Lift it out.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Do this properly or skip it altogether. A pale, crowded sear gives you grey meat and twenty wasted minutes.</span>

**3.** Into the same pot for 10 minutes:

{{< ingr "onions=2, carrots=3, celery=2 sticks, garlic=4 cloves" >}}

**4.** Tomato puree for a minute, then the wine. Scrape the bottom of the pot and let it bubble down by half.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">The scraping is the point. Everything you got from the browning is stuck to the bottom.</span>

**5.** Beef back in with the meat mostly submerged, then lid on, into the oven for 3 hours:

{{< ingr "beef stock=500ml, bay leaves=2, thyme=few sprigs" >}}

**6.** Potatoes in for the last hour.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Any earlier and they collapse. Same for any root veg.</span>

**7.** Check the meat pulls apart with a spoon before it comes out. Salt.

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin:0.2em 0 0.9em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Go by the meat, not the clock. Overcooked shin tightens back up and goes tough again.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 2px solid #E5DECF; padding-left: 0.6em;">Shin is full of connective tissue, and three hours low turns that collagen into gelatin. That is what thickens the sauce and makes it glossy.<br>Better the next day.</span>

<span style="display:block; color:#888; font-size:0.8em; margin-top:0.8em; border-left: 3px solid #8fcfe8; padding-left: 0.6em;">❄ <strong>Freezer</strong><br>Freeze: cool fully, then freeze in portions for up to 3 months. Leave the potatoes out if you plan to freeze it.<br>Reheat: from frozen, low oven or a gentle pan until piping hot.</span>
